Welcome, classroom explorer!

R.E.A.C.H. is an inclusive education advocacy campaign focused on breaking barriers and helping every learner belong, participate, and thrive—because every learner deserves a chance to belong and succeed.

Target audience

Teachers

Learning outcomes

  • Identify a participation barrier.
  • Explain the five R.E.A.C.H. pillars.
  • Justify an inclusive response using RA 11650.
  • Plan one practical classroom action.

Your route

Read the issue, explore the pillars, try the cases, draft an action plan, and make a commitment.

What barrier can I remove so that a learner can participate more meaningfully?

Understand the issue

The same classroom does not always create the same experience. Although policies protect learners’ right to education, enrollment alone does not guarantee appropriate materials, accessible facilities, individualized support, or meaningful classroom participation.

The R.E.A.C.H. pathway

Select each pillar to explore its meaning and a classroom reflection question.

Knowledge check

A teacher adapts materials and provides appropriate alternative ways for learners to demonstrate understanding. Which pillar most directly describes this practice?

04 · 2 minutes

Make inclusive classroom decisions

Choose the most appropriate response in each scenario and review the feedback.

Case 1: The Unheard Learner

A Deaf learner cannot fully access a lesson because the teacher faces the board while speaking and gives only oral instructions.

Case 2: The Inaccessible Worksheet

A learner with low vision cannot read the small print on a science worksheet about the water cycle.

Key takeaway: Identify barriers, listen to learners, and adapt instruction without unnecessarily lowering learning expectations.

06 · 1 minute

REACH ONE. REMOVE ONE.

One learner. One barrier. One meaningful action.

Example: Remove a communication barrier

Before my next lesson, I will provide written or visual instructions alongside spoken instructions, face the class while speaking, and ask the learner which communication supports work best. I will observe whether these adjustments improve access to instructions and participation.
